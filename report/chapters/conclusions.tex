\section{Conclusions}

The project shows that the robot movement planning problem can be expressed
compactly as a Constraint Programming model by separating it into grouping and
scheduling decisions. Representing each group through its start index and
length avoids the larger fully enumerated and binary-membership encodings,
while still capturing the geometric constraints of the gripper. The release
schedule can then be described with a small number of ordering variables and
local neighbor constraints, which keeps the model readable and close to the
physical interpretation of the task.

The benchmark confirms that the CP-oriented solvers scale well across
realistic pallet sizes within the 5-minute budget. Larger grippers reduce the
number of groups to schedule and generally make the instances easier, while
dense layouts with many small boxes remain the most demanding cases.
\texttt{gecode} is the most stable solver in these experiments,
\texttt{cp-sat} and \texttt{chuffed} remain competitive, and \texttt{highs}
exposes the cost of using a linear representation for this kind of combinatorial
model. The comparison between wall time and solver-reported time also shows
that, for very fast runs, MiniZinc flattening and process startup become a
visible part of the total runtime.

Overall, the adopted formulation is a practical baseline for generating valid
pick-and-place plans. Possible extensions include adding an objective function,
for example to minimize placement time or favor smoother robot motions, and
testing additional search annotations or symmetry-breaking constraints on the
hardest dense instances.
